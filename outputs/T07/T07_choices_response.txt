## 1. Analytical choices to make before plotting

The question sounds simple, but the answer depends on how we define and measure COVID-19 deaths.

| Analytical choice            | Option chosen                                                        | Why                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Measure of deaths            | Change in `total_deaths`                                             | This column records cumulative reported deaths. Taking the difference between year-end totals estimates the deaths added during each year and avoids relying on the sum of daily reports. |
| Totals or rates              | Absolute number of deaths                                            | The question asks which year had more deaths. Rates such as `total_deaths_per_million` are more appropriate for comparing the risk between populations of different sizes.                |
| Time period                  | Calendar years 2021 and 2022                                         | Compare December 31, 2020, with December 31, 2021, and December 31, 2022. This captures the change in cumulative deaths during each calendar year.                                        |
| Rows to include              | Only rows with `iso_code == "USA"` and `location == "United States"` | The dataset includes countries, continents, and global aggregates. Filtering to the United States prevents accidentally including other populations or aggregate rows.                    |
| Missing data                 | Require usable cumulative death totals on all three year-end dates   | The calculation needs three snapshots. Rather than silently substitute different dates, the code will stop and flag missing endpoint data.                                                |
| Daily versus smoothed counts | Use `total_deaths`, not `new_deaths` or `new_deaths_smoothed`        | Daily reports can contain missing values, retrospective corrections, and reporting-day fluctuations. The cumulative measure provides a straightforward year-to-year comparison.           |

### What could make the chart misleading?

There are several important limitations to keep in mind:

- Reported deaths are not necessarily deaths that occurred on that exact date. Reporting delays, backlogs, and retrospective revisions can affect the cumulative series.
- Cumulative figures can be revised. The difference between two year-end values measures the net increase in reported cumulative deaths, which may not perfectly match the number of deaths that actually occurred during the year.
- Absolute counts do not adjust for population size. This is appropriate for answering which year had more deaths in the United States, but not for comparing mortality rates between countries.
- The chart does not explain why the numbers changed. It compares two annual totals; it does not establish the causes of differences.

Calculation:

\\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Deaths in 2021} &= T\_{2021}-T\_{2020}\\\ \text{Deaths in 2022} &= T\_{2022}-T\_{2021} \end{aligned} \\]

Here, \\(T_y\\) is the cumulative `total_deaths` value on December 31 of year \\(y\\).

## 3. How to interpret the chart

Each bar represents the increase in cumulative reported COVID-19 deaths during one calendar year. The taller bar indicates the year with more reported deaths added.

The code prints the calculated totals and the comparison in the console, so you can use the output to answer the question directly.

One final caution: this method measures the increase in the dataset's reported cumulative death count between year-end snapshots. It is a transparent way to compare years, but it should not be described as a perfectly exact count of the dates on which deaths occurred.
