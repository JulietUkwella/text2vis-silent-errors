## 1. Analytical choices and why

The question asks whether European countries with more hospital beds tend to have fewer COVID-19 deaths. I would use a scatter plot, with hospital bed capacity on the horizontal axis and cumulative COVID-19 deaths per million people on the vertical axis.

| Analytical choice                 | Option chosen                                                                                                             | Reason                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Measure of hospital capacity      | `hospital_beds_per_thousand`                                                                                              | Measures hospital beds per 1,000 people, making comparisons between countries of different sizes more meaningful than using raw bed counts.                                      |
| Measure of COVID-19 deaths        | `total_deaths_per_million`                                                                                                | Measures cumulative deaths per million people, rather than total deaths, so large countries do not automatically dominate the comparison.                                        |
| Totals or rates                   | Rates for both variables                                                                                                  | Population-adjusted measures are more appropriate for comparing countries of different sizes.                                                                                    |
| Time period                       | Latest date available in the European rows of the CSV                                                                     | Provides the most recent overall snapshot in the supplied file.                                                                                                                  |
| Consistency of death observations | Use each country's latest non-missing death rate on or before the reference date, provided it is no more than 90 days old | Reduces the risk of comparing a recent observation from one country with a substantially older observation from another. The 90-day threshold is an explicit, adjustable choice. |
| Hospital bed observation          | Latest non-missing bed-capacity value available on or before the reference date                                           | Uses the most recently available value in the file, without requiring bed capacity and deaths to be present in exactly the same row.                                             |
| Geographic inclusion              | Rows where `continent == "Europe"`                                                                                        | Uses the dataset's own geographic classification and excludes most non-country aggregates that lack a continent assignment.                                                      |
| Missing data                      | Exclude countries missing either required measure                                                                         | Avoids imputing values that could create artificial relationships.                                                                                                               |
| Chart type                        | Labelled scatter plot with a simple trend line                                                                            | Each point represents a country. The trend line helps reveal a possible association but does not establish causation.                                                            |

### What could make the chart misleading?

Several limitations matter when interpreting the results:

- Hospital beds are not necessarily COVID-19 beds. `hospital_beds_per_thousand` measures general hospital capacity. It does not indicate how many beds were staffed, available during a surge, or equipped for intensive care.
- The measurement years may differ. Hospital bed capacity can come from an older source year, and the listed columns do not identify the original measurement year. The latest row containing a value does not necessarily mean that the capacity was measured recently.
- Death observations can be from different dates. The code uses a common reference date but accepts a country's last valid death observation if it is within 90 days of that date. The actual observation dates will therefore not necessarily match.
- Reported deaths are imperfect. Countries differ in how they define, attribute, and report COVID-19 deaths. Underreporting and revisions can affect comparisons.
- Cumulative deaths do not measure hospital pressure directly. A country's death rate is influenced by age structure, vaccination, population health, variants, public-health measures, and many other factors, not simply hospital bed capacity.
- The geographic selection follows OWID's labels. If a strict comparison of sovereign states is required, the included locations should be reviewed for territories or other special cases.

Consequently, the chart can show whether hospital bed capacity is associated with cumulative COVID-19 deaths, but it cannot establish that more beds caused a country to have fewer deaths.

## 3. How to interpret the chart

Each dot represents a European location, with its hospital bed capacity on the x-axis and cumulative COVID-19 deaths per million on the y-axis.

- Dots toward the right indicate greater reported hospital bed capacity per capita.
- Dots higher up indicate more reported cumulative COVID-19 deaths per capita.
- A downward-sloping trend line would indicate that greater hospital bed capacity is associated with fewer cumulative deaths in this cross-country comparison.
- An upward-sloping line would indicate a positive association, while a nearly flat line would indicate little linear association.

These are possible interpretations, not results claimed in advance. The actual pattern depends on the contents of your CSV file.

One final consideration: the 90-day exclusion rule deliberately prioritizes recent and reasonably comparable death observations over maximum country coverage. The code prints excluded locations so you can see its impact. For a chart intended to represent a specific period, setting `REFERENCE_DATE` to a fixed historical date is preferable to relying on the file's latest date.
