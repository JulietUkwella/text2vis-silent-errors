## 1. Analytical choices to make before plotting

The question “Which African countries were most affected by COVID-19?” does not have a single definitive answer. A country could have a high number of deaths overall but a lower death rate per person, while another could have a relatively small total number of deaths but a much higher death rate.

For this chart, I would define “most affected” as the African countries with the highest reported cumulative COVID-19 deaths per million people. This focuses on the reported mortality burden relative to population size.

| Analytical choice         | Option chosen                                                                   | Why                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Measure of impact         | `total_deaths_per_million`                                                      | Deaths provide an indicator of severe outcomes, although they do not capture every aspect of the pandemic.                                                   |
| Totals or rates?          | Rate per million people                                                         | Makes comparisons between countries of different population sizes more meaningful. It does not show which countries had the most deaths in absolute numbers. |
| Time period               | Latest non-missing observation for each country in the file                     | Uses the most recent reported cumulative death rate available in the supplied CSV. The data dates will be shown alongside country names.                     |
| Countries to include      | Rows where `continent == "Africa"` and the ISO code does not begin with `OWID_` | Focuses on African countries and excludes OWID regional or other aggregate series, which should not be ranked as countries.                                  |
| Rows to include           | One latest valid observation per country                                        | `total_deaths_per_million` is cumulative, so summing it across daily rows would incorrectly count the same deaths repeatedly.                                |
| Missing data              | Exclude countries without a valid death-rate observation                        | Missing values cannot support a ranking. Countries excluded for this reason should not be interpreted as having zero deaths.                                 |
| Number of countries shown | Top 10                                                                          | Makes the chart readable and focuses attention on the highest reported rates.                                                                                |
| Chart type                | Horizontal bar chart, ordered from lowest to highest within the top 10          | Makes country names easy to read and supports direct comparison.                                                                                             |

### What could make this chart misleading?

- Different reporting dates: The latest available observation may be from a different date for each country. The code will display the date used for each bar, but the comparison is not a perfectly synchronized snapshot.
- Underreporting and inconsistent definitions: Some COVID-19 deaths may not have been identified or reported. Countries can differ in death registration, reporting practices, and how COVID-related deaths are classified.
- Population adjustment is not the whole story: Deaths per million measures reported mortality relative to population, not the total number of deaths, the proportion infected, or the strain on hospitals.
- Data completeness: Countries with missing or incomplete reporting may be omitted or appear less affected than they actually were.
- Country size and statistical instability: Rates can be more volatile in countries with small populations. Revisions to relatively few reported deaths can noticeably change their rankings.
- Cumulative figures can be revised: The latest reported value is not necessarily a final or fully comparable estimate of the true pandemic toll.

A different question would require a different chart. For example, `total_deaths` would show the largest absolute death tolls, while `total_cases_per_million` would compare reported infection rates. The latter is especially sensitive to differences in testing and case detection.

## 3. How to interpret the result

The chart identifies the 10 countries with the highest reported cumulative COVID-19 deaths per million people, based on the latest non-missing observation available for each country in the CSV.

Keep three points in mind when drawing conclusions:

1. A high rate is not the same as the highest total toll. A country with a smaller population may rank highly per million while recording fewer deaths overall.
2. The dates matter. Check the dates printed beside the country names. For a strict, date-for-date comparison, rerun the analysis using a common cutoff date and include only countries with valid observations on or before that date.
3. The ranking reflects reported data, not necessarily the true mortality burden. Underreporting, differences in death registration, and missing observations can change the results.

Recommended next step for a broader answer: make a second chart using `total_deaths` to identify the countries with the largest absolute reported death tolls. Comparing that chart with the per-million ranking would distinguish the largest overall impact from the highest population-adjusted mortality.
