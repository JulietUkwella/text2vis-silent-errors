The code below ranks countries by their average daily percentage growth in cumulative confirmed COVID-19 cases during March 2020 and plots the 10 fastest-growing countries. It uses a starting threshold of at least 100 cases to reduce the effect of very small initial counts and excludes OWID aggregate rows such as continents and the world.

```
import pandas as pdimport matplotlib.pyplot as plt# 1. Load the datasetdf = pd.read_csv("owid-covid-data.csv", parse_dates=["date"])# 2. Select country-level data for March 2020march = df.loc[    df["date"].between("2020-03-01", "2020-03-31")    & df["continent"].notna()    & ~df["iso_code"].fillna("").str.startswith("OWID_"),    ["iso_code", "location", "date", "total_cases"]].copy()march["total_cases"] = pd.to_numeric(    march["total_cases"], errors="coerce")march = march.dropna(    subset=["iso_code", "total_cases"]).sort_values("date")# 3. Get the first and last available case counts for each countrystats = (    march.groupby("iso_code")    .agg(        country=("location", "last"),        start_date=("date", "first"),        start_cases=("total_cases", "first"),        end_date=("date", "last"),        end_cases=("total_cases", "last")    )    .reset_index())# Calculate the number of days coveredstats["days"] = (    stats["end_date"] - stats["start_date"]).dt.days# Retain countries with a meaningful starting count and# observations spanning at least 25 daysstats = stats[    (stats["start_cases"] >= 100)    & (stats["days"] >= 25)    & (stats["end_cases"] > stats["start_cases"])].copy()# 4. Calculate compound average daily growth rate (%)stats["daily_growth_pct"] = (    (stats["end_cases"] / stats["start_cases"])    ** (1 / stats["days"]) - 1) * 100# Select the 10 countries with the fastest growthtop10 = stats.nlargest(10, "daily_growth_pct")top10 = top10.sort_values("daily_growth_pct")# 5. Print the rankingprint("Countries with the fastest relative case growth in March 2020:")print(    top10.sort_values("daily_growth_pct", ascending=False)[        ["country", "start_cases", "end_cases", "daily_growth_pct"]    ].to_string(        index=False,        formatters={"daily_growth_pct": "{:.2f}%".format}    ))# 6. Create a horizontal bar chartfig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 6))bars = ax.barh(    top10["country"],    top10["daily_growth_pct"])ax.set_title(    "Countries with the Fastest COVID-19 Case Growth\n"    "March 2020",    fontsize=14)ax.set_xlabel("Average compound daily growth in confirmed cases (%)")ax.set_ylabel("Country")ax.grid(axis="x", linestyle="--", alpha=0.4)ax.set_axisbelow(True)# Display growth rates at the ends of the barsfor bar in bars:    width = bar.get_width()    ax.text(        width + 0.1,        bar.get_y() + bar.get_height() / 2,        f"{width:.1f}%",        va="center"    )plt.tight_layout()plt.show()
```

### How to interpret the chart

- Longer bars represent countries with faster relative daily growth in confirmed cumulative cases.
- The printed table shows each country's first and last observed case counts during March and its estimated daily growth rate.
- The starting threshold and minimum observation period improve comparability, but mean that countries with fewer than 100 cases at their first March observation may be excluded.

Important distinction: This measures relative growth, not the largest absolute increase in cases. A country with a smaller outbreak can grow faster in percentage terms than a country that reports many more new cases. The actual country ranking will be calculated from your local CSV when you run the code.
