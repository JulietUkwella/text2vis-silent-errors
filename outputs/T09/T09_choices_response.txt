## 1. Analytical choices before writing the code

The question is whether COVID-19 deaths in the United Kingdom fell after vaccines were rolled out. To answer it fairly, the chart should show deaths over time, identify when vaccination began, and avoid implying that a change in deaths was necessarily caused by vaccines.

### Choices and rationale

| Analytical choice          | Option selected                                         | Why                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Measure of deaths          | `new_deaths_smoothed`                                   | Measures daily deaths averaged over seven days. This makes the overall trend easier to see than highly variable daily counts.                                         |
| Deaths or death rates      | Absolute daily deaths                                   | The question concerns deaths in the UK, so actual numbers are intuitive. Since we are examining one country over time, comparing deaths per million is not essential. |
| New deaths or total deaths | New deaths, not `total_deaths`                          | Cumulative deaths generally increase or stay flat, even when daily deaths decline. Daily deaths answer the question more directly.                                    |
| Time period                | All available UK records                                | Showing the full timeline provides context about deaths before vaccination, the initial rollout, and subsequent waves.                                                |
| Vaccination reference date | 8 December 2020                                         | Mark the date the UK's first COVID-19 vaccination programme began, so the reader can distinguish the pre-rollout and post-rollout periods.                            |
| Rows to include            | Rows where `location == "United Kingdom"`               | This avoids mixing UK figures with other countries or regional rows.                                                                                                  |
| Missing observations       | Exclude rows with missing dates or death measurements   | Missing values should not be interpreted as zero deaths.                                                                                                              |
| Smoothing                  | Use the dataset's seven-day smoothed measure            | This reduces noise from weekday reporting patterns and makes trends easier to interpret.                                                                              |
| Chart format               | A time-series line chart with a vertical rollout marker | This makes it easy to compare the trend before and after vaccination began.                                                                                           |

The rollout date is a reference point, not a claim that large numbers of people were protected immediately. The impact of vaccination would depend on how quickly people received doses and developed protection.

### What could make the chart misleading?

- Correlation is not causation. A drop after rollout would not prove that vaccines caused the drop. Lockdowns, seasonal patterns, new variants, changes in behaviour, and improvements in treatment could also affect deaths.
- The rollout took time. The first vaccine was administered on 8 December 2020, but vaccination coverage increased gradually. A vertical line cannot represent when the whole population gained protection. [UK government source](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-marks-one-year-since-deploying-worlds-first-covid-19-vaccine?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
- Reported deaths are not necessarily all COVID-19 deaths. Definitions, reporting practices, and death attribution can vary. The Our World in Data documentation notes these limitations. [OWID data documentation](https://github.com/owid/covid-19-data/blob/master/public/data/README.md?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
- Later waves matter. Deaths might initially fall after the rollout and rise again later. Looking at only a short post-rollout window could give an incomplete picture.
- Missing data and revisions matter. Excluding missing observations is appropriate, but the chart will reflect the data available in your saved CSV, which may not be the latest version.

## 2. Python code

The following code loads your CSV, filters for the United Kingdom, and plots seven-day smoothed daily COVID-19 deaths. The vertical dashed line marks the start of the UK vaccination rollout.

```
import pandas as pdimport matplotlib.pyplot as plt# 1. Load the datasetfile_path = "owid-covid-data.csv"df = pd.read_csv(file_path)# 2. Select only the United Kingdom's recordsuk = df.loc[    df["location"] == "United Kingdom",    ["date", "new_deaths_smoothed"]].copy()# 3. Convert columns to suitable data typesuk["date"] = pd.to_datetime(uk["date"], errors="coerce")uk["new_deaths_smoothed"] = pd.to_numeric(    uk["new_deaths_smoothed"], errors="coerce")# 4. Remove missing observations and sort chronologicallyuk = (    uk.dropna(subset=["date", "new_deaths_smoothed"])      .sort_values("date"))if uk.empty:    raise ValueError(        "No valid United Kingdom death data was found in the CSV."    )# 5. Set the date of the first UK vaccinationrollout_date = pd.Timestamp("2020-12-08")# 6. Create the chartfig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(12, 6))ax.plot(    uk["date"],    uk["new_deaths_smoothed"],    linewidth=1.8,    label="Daily deaths (7-day smoothed)")# Mark the start of the vaccination rolloutax.axvline(    rollout_date,    color="red",    linestyle="--",    linewidth=1.5,    label="UK vaccination rollout begins")# Add a label near the top of the chartax.text(    rollout_date + pd.Timedelta(days=20),    0.96,    "First vaccine: 8 Dec 2020",    transform=ax.get_xaxis_transform(),    color="red",    verticalalignment="top")# 7. Add titles and labelsax.set_title(    "Did COVID-19 Deaths Fall After Vaccination Began in the UK?")ax.set_xlabel("Date")ax.set_ylabel("Daily COVID-19 deaths (7-day average)")ax.grid(axis="y", alpha=0.3)ax.legend()fig.autofmt_xdate()plt.tight_layout()plt.show()
```

## 3. How to interpret the chart

Look at the death trend on either side of the dashed line.

If smoothed daily deaths decline during the months after 8 December 2020, that supports the descriptive conclusion that reported COVID-19 deaths fell after vaccination began. If deaths rise during later waves, that is also important evidence to include in the interpretation.

The chart alone cannot establish how much of any decline was caused by vaccination. A stronger follow-up analysis would compare deaths with vaccination coverage over time, accounting for a delay between vaccination and its expected effect on mortality.
